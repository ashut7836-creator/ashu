Uranus

A guide to Uranus

Uranus is an ice giant with a pale blue-green atmosphere. Its most unusual feature is an extreme tilt: it rotates almost on its side as it travels around the Sun.

Mean radius25,400 km
Average distance from Sun2.9 billion km
Rotation period17.2 hours
Orbital period84 Earth years
Temperature≈ −195 °C
Planet typeIce giant

Atmosphere

Its atmosphere is mostly hydrogen and helium with methane, which absorbs red light and contributes to the cyan colour. It is extremely cold.

Surface and composition

There is no solid surface. Beneath the clouds is a deep, hot, dense mixture of water, ammonia and methane around a small rocky core.

Rotation and orbit

Uranus is tilted about 98 degrees, so it rolls along its orbit on its side. Each pole experiences decades of continuous sunlight and darkness.

Moons and rings

Uranus has many known moons, including Miranda, Ariel, Titania and Oberon. It also has a set of narrow, dark rings.

Space exploration

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to have visited Uranus, flying past in 1986. A dedicated orbiter is a leading idea for a future mission.

Quick facts about Uranus

  • Rotates almost on its side
  • Has narrow dark rings
  • Visited by Voyager 2 in 1986
