Solar system field guide

Meet the eight planets.

Explore the rocky inner worlds and the giant planets farther from the Sun. Each guide explains its atmosphere, surface or composition, orbit, moons and space missions in clear language.

Mercury

Terrestrial planet

Mercury is the smallest planet and the closest to the Sun. Its heavily cratered surface resembles Earth's Moon, but its large iron-rich core makes it unusually dense.

Read Mercury guide →

Venus

Terrestrial planet

Venus is nearly as large as Earth, but its environment is extreme. Thick clouds hide the surface, while a powerful greenhouse effect makes it the hottest planet.

Read Venus guide →

Earth

Terrestrial planet

Earth is our home and the only world currently known to support life. Liquid-water oceans, a protective atmosphere and a magnetic field help make its surface habitable.

Read Earth guide →

Mars

Terrestrial planet

Mars is a cold desert world with rusty-red dust, giant volcanoes and deep canyons. Ancient river channels and lake beds suggest that liquid water once flowed across its surface.

Read Mars guide →

Jupiter

Gas giant

Jupiter is the largest planet. It is so massive that all the other planets together still contain less mass. Jupiter is a gas giant with no solid surface to stand on.

Read Jupiter guide →

Saturn

Gas giant

Saturn is a gas giant best known for its spectacular ring system. It is the least dense planet on average, and its pale bands are swept by powerful winds.

Read Saturn guide →

Uranus

Ice giant

Uranus is an ice giant with a pale blue-green atmosphere. Its most unusual feature is an extreme tilt: it rotates almost on its side as it travels around the Sun.

Read Uranus guide →

Neptune

Ice giant

Neptune is the most distant planet, a deep-blue ice giant. It was the first planet located through mathematical prediction before it was seen through a telescope in 1846.

Read Neptune guide →

Planet sizes in illustrations are not to scale. Distances, temperatures and rotation figures are rounded reference values; some giant-planet temperatures refer to cloud tops.
