Neptune

A guide to Neptune

Neptune is the most distant planet, a deep-blue ice giant. It was the first planet located through mathematical prediction before it was seen through a telescope in 1846.

Mean radius24,600 km
Average distance from Sun4.5 billion km
Rotation period16.1 hours
Orbital period165 Earth years
Temperature≈ −200 °C
Planet typeIce giant

Atmosphere

Its atmosphere contains hydrogen, helium and methane, with dark storms and bright clouds. Winds can exceed 2,000 kilometres per hour.

Surface and composition

Neptune has no solid surface. Like Uranus, it is rich in water, ammonia and methane under its visible clouds.

Rotation and orbit

Neptune takes about 165 Earth years to orbit once, so it has completed only about one orbit since its discovery.

Moons and rings

Neptune has several known moons. Triton is the largest, follows a backwards orbit and has active nitrogen geysers. Neptune also has faint rings.

Space exploration

Voyager 2 is the only spacecraft to visit Neptune, flying past in 1989. Most later observations come from powerful space and ground telescopes.

Quick facts about Neptune

  • Farthest planet from the Sun
  • Has extremely fast winds
  • Discovered after mathematical predictions
